# Food-safety reference

Authoritative standards for judging whether a recipe is *safe to cook and eat as written*. Food-safety problems are the most serious class of finding — a recipe that tells you to undercook chicken or slow-cook raw kidney beans is dangerous regardless of how good it tastes. Primary sources: USDA FSIS, FDA, CDC, foodsafety.gov, and the National Center for Home Food Preservation (NCHFP).

## 1. Safe minimum internal cooking temperatures (USDA FSIS)

Doneness by color/texture is **not** a safety indicator — only a thermometer in the thickest part confirms it. This is the canonical table; a meat/poultry/seafood/egg recipe should target these (or give an unambiguous doneness cue).

| Food | Safe minimum internal temp | Rest |
|---|---|---|
| **Poultry** — all, whole or ground (chicken, turkey, duck); stuffing; breasts/thighs/wings | **165 °F** | none |
| **Ground meats** — beef, pork, lamb, veal | **160 °F** | none |
| **Beef / pork / veal / lamb** — steaks, chops, roasts (whole cuts) | **145 °F** | **3 min** |
| **Fresh pork / ham (raw)** | **145 °F** | **3 min** |
| **Ham, fully cooked** (reheat, commercially packaged) | **140 °F** | none |
| **Ham, fully cooked, repackaged/leftover** | **165 °F** | none |
| **Eggs** | cook until white **and** yolk are firm | none |
| **Egg dishes** (casserole, quiche, frittata) | **160 °F** | none |
| **Fish & finfish** | **145 °F**, or flesh opaque and flakes | none |
| Shrimp / lobster / crab / scallops | until flesh is pearly/opaque | — |
| Clams / oysters / mussels | until shells open (discard any that stay shut) | — |
| **Leftovers & casseroles** (reheat) | **165 °F** | none |
| **Hot-holding** cooked food for service | **140 °F or above** | n/a |

Ground meats stay hotter (160 °F, poultry 165 °F) than whole cuts (145 °F) because grinding spreads surface bacteria throughout. Whole cuts got the 145 °F + 3-min-rest harmonization in 2011 (pork dropped from 160 °F).

Sources: [FSIS Safe Minimum Internal Temperature Chart](https://www.fsis.usda.gov/food-safety/safe-food-handling-and-preparation/food-safety-basics/safe-temperature-chart) · [foodsafety.gov](https://www.foodsafety.gov/food-safety-charts/safe-minimum-internal-temperatures) · [FSIS Leftovers and Food Safety](https://www.fsis.usda.gov/food-safety/safe-food-handling-and-preparation/food-safety-basics/leftovers-and-food-safety)

## 2. The Danger Zone + time/thawing rules

- **Danger Zone = 40–140 °F.** Bacteria can double in ~20 min here. Keep cold food ≤ 40 °F, hot food ≥ 140 °F.
- **2-hour rule:** never leave perishables (meat, poultry, eggs, seafood, cooked dishes, cut produce, dairy) out of refrigeration > 2 hours total; **1 hour if ambient > 90 °F.**
- **Cooling leftovers:** refrigerate within 2 hours in **shallow** containers; don't cool a large hot pot on the counter or fridge a big sealed mass.
- **Safe thawing — three methods only** (never on the counter): refrigerator; cold water in a leak-proof bag changed every 30 min (cook immediately); microwave (cook immediately). Counter/room-temp thawing is a red flag.

Sources: [FSIS Danger Zone](https://www.fsis.usda.gov/food-safety/safe-food-handling-and-preparation/food-safety-basics/danger-zone-40f-140f) · [FSIS The Big Thaw](https://www.fsis.usda.gov/food-safety/safe-food-handling-and-preparation/food-safety-basics/big-thaw-safe-defrosting-methods)

## 3. Ingredients / preparations dangerous if done wrong

Format: **ingredient → hazard → safe rule.** These are *critical* findings when a recipe violates the rule.

- **Raw/undercooked red kidney beans** (and similar beans) → phytohaemagglutinin (a lectin); as few as 4–5 raw beans cause vomiting/diarrhea in 1–3 hrs. → **Soak, discard water, boil at a full rolling boil ≥ 10 min** (30 min to be sure). **Never cook raw dried kidney beans in a slow cooker** — low temps can *raise* toxicity up to 5×. [FDA Bad Bug Book](https://www.fda.gov/food/foodborne-pathogens/bad-bug-book-second-edition)
- **Green/sprouted potatoes** → solanine/chaconine (glycoalkaloids); cooking does **not** destroy them. → **Discard heavily green/sprouted potatoes; cut away green, eyes, sprouts; store cool + dark.**
- **Raw elderberries** (also leaves/stems/unripe) → cyanogenic glycosides. → **Cook/boil fully before use; never raw.**
- **Cassava (yuca), raw/under-processed** → cyanogenic glycosides (linamarin → cyanide). → **Peel, soak, cook thoroughly;** bitter varieties need extended soaking/fermentation. Never raw.
- **Unripe ackee** → hypoglycin A ("Jamaican vomiting sickness," can be fatal). → **Only the naturally-opened ripe fruit; discard seeds + pink membrane.**
- **Rhubarb leaves** → oxalic acid/oxalates; cooking doesn't detoxify. → **Stalks only; discard all leaves.**
- **Raw/undercooked eggs** → Salmonella. → **Cook until firm / egg dishes to 160 °F.** For raw/lightly-cooked-egg recipes (Caesar dressing, homemade mayo, mousse, eggnog, meringue) → **use pasteurized shell eggs or pasteurized egg product.** [FDA Egg Safety](https://www.fda.gov/food/buy-store-serve-safe-food/what-you-need-know-about-egg-safety)
- **Unpasteurized (raw) milk / raw-milk cheese** → Salmonella, Listeria, E. coli O157:H7, Campylobacter. → **Use pasteurized dairy** (critical for pregnant, young, elderly, immunocompromised). [CDC Raw Milk](https://www.cdc.gov/food-safety/foods/raw-milk.html)
- **Unpasteurized juice/cider** → E. coli O157:H7, Salmonella, Cryptosporidium. → **Use pasteurized, or heat-treat fresh cider.**
- **Garlic/herbs stored in oil at room temperature** → Clostridium botulinum (anaerobic, low-acid) → botulism. → **Refrigerate and use within 4 days**, or acidify per a tested recipe / freeze. Never store at room temp. [CDC Botulism](https://www.cdc.gov/botulism/prevention/home-canned-foods.html)
- **Home-canning low-acid foods** (vegetables, meats, poultry, fish, most mixtures, pH > 4.6) → botulism; home-canned vegetables are the top U.S. botulism cause. → **Pressure canner required** (240–250 °F); a boiling-water bath (212 °F) won't kill the spores. **Use only research-tested recipes** (USDA/NCHFP/extension). [NCHFP](https://nchfp.uga.edu/how/can/general-information/for-safetys-sake/)
  - **Acidity is the deciding factor — don't reflexively flag every canning recipe.** **High-acid foods (pH ≤ 4.6)** are correctly and safely processed in a **boiling-water bath**: pickles and vinegar brines, most fruit and fruit butters (apple butter, jams, jellies, most fruit), fruit juices, and *properly acidified* tomatoes/salsas/relishes (recipe adds vinegar/lemon/citric acid to a tested ratio). The scanner flags any canning recipe as a **warning** (it can't measure pH); **read the method and judge**: high-acid + boiling-water bath = correct (not a finding); low-acid + boiling-water bath = the critical error above. Still verify the recipe follows a **tested** ratio/process and adjusts time for **altitude** (>1,000 ft).
- **Reused marinade that touched raw meat** → cross-contamination. → **Discard, or boil to a rolling boil before serving;** or reserve a clean portion before marinating.
- **Cross-contamination: raw meat → ready-to-eat** → **Separate boards/utensils; wash hands + surfaces between tasks; never plate cooked food on a dish that held raw meat.**
- **Undercooked pork / wild game** → trichinella (rare in U.S. commercial pork; persists in game/bear). → **Whole cuts 145 °F + rest, ground 160 °F, wild game 160 °F.**
- **Nutmeg in large doses** → myristicin (hallucinogenic/toxic); ~2 tsp (~10 g) can cause serious symptoms. → **Culinary pinch-to-½-tsp is fine; flag tablespoons-per-serving.**

## 4. FDA "Big 9" major allergens (for labeling-completeness checks)

**Milk · Eggs · Fish** (declare species) **· Crustacean shellfish** (declare type) **· Tree nuts** (declare type) **· Peanuts · Wheat · Soybeans · Sesame** (the 9th, effective Jan 1 2023, FASTER Act). Mollusks (clam/oyster/scallop) are *not* Big 9 but are common allergens worth noting. These 9 cause ~90% of food allergies. A recipe's declared `allergens:` should cover every Big-9 ingredient actually present. [FDA Food Allergies](https://www.fda.gov/food/nutrition-food-labeling-and-critical-foods/food-allergies) · [FASTER Act — sesame](https://www.fda.gov/food/food-allergies/faster-act-sesame-ninth-major-food-allergen)

**When the recipe has no `allergens:` field at all** (many clipping-format recipes), the scanner emits a single *note*, not per-allergen warnings — you can't be "missing" from a list that doesn't exist. Treat that as informational: only push it to a warning if this collection is *supposed* to carry allergen labels. A genuine labeling gap is when the field **exists but omits a Big-9 ingredient that's present** (e.g. Pad Thai declaring "nut" but not "peanut"; a burger declaring gluten/dairy but not the egg in its mayo).

**Keyword "false friends" — sanity-check these scanner allergen hits before repeating them** (the scanner masks the clearest ones but leaves judgment calls in):
- **Not the allergen:** "butter beans"/"butter lettuce" (not dairy), plant milks/butters ("coconut/almond/oat milk", "peanut butter" is *peanut* not dairy), "cream of tartar", "oyster mushroom" (not shellfish), "water chestnut" (not a nut), "nutmeg" (not a tree nut), "coconut" (FDA lists it as a tree nut, but many label it separately).
- **Trace / derived — real but often a judgment call:** **Worcestershire sauce** contains anchovy (→ fish); **soy sauce** is brewed with wheat (→ gluten) unless it's tamari; **fish sauce** (→ fish). Flag when the dish otherwise claims to be allergen-free or the amount is non-trivial.
- **Refined oils:** highly-refined **peanut** and **soybean oil** are exempt from FDA allergen labeling; cold-pressed/gourmet/expeller-pressed versions are **not**. If the only "peanut" is refined frying oil, note it rather than failing.

## 5. Technique red flags (quick scan)

- Meat/poultry cooked below its safe minimum, or with a color/time cue and **no temperature** ("cook chicken until golden," burgers "to taste").
- Poultry target < 165 °F, ground meat < 160 °F, whole cuts < 145 °F.
- **Marinating at room temperature** (must be refrigerated); reusing raw-meat marinade unboiled.
- **Unsafe cooling** — "cool the big pot on the counter overnight," fridging a large dense mass whole.
- Perishables held/served at room temp beyond the 2-hour (1-hour > 90 °F) limit; overnight room-temp rise of egg/dairy batters.
- Raw dried kidney beans in a slow cooker / beans not brought to a vigorous boil.
- Raw/lightly-cooked eggs without specifying pasteurized; unpasteurized milk/cheese/juice as an ingredient.
- Garlic/herb-in-oil with no refrigeration/use-by note; infused oils stored at room temp.
- Home canning of low-acid foods by boiling-water bath, or canning not from a tested source.
- Counter/room-temp thawing; no hand-wash / separate-board step between raw meat and RTE assembly.
- Stuffing cooked inside poultry without verifying the stuffing center hits 165 °F.
- Toxic-if-raw plant parts used raw/under-processed; grossly excessive doses of a culinary toxicant.
