# Writing about religion

Readers of any tradition, and of none, should be able to read the piece and find their own
tradition described fairly and no tradition promoted or attacked. Three habits do most of
the work.

## 1. Attribute faith claims

Say whose claim it is, in the sentence: "Christians believe Jesus rose from the dead",
"Muslims regard the Qur'an as the word of God", "In Hindu tradition, Krishna is an avatar of
Vishnu". Historical claims get the same treatment from the other side: "Most historians
think…". The writer's voice states only what isn't in dispute.

Name the branch when it matters: "Catholics" or "many Protestants" rather than
"Christians", "Orthodox Jews" rather than "Jews", "Theravada Buddhists" rather than
"Buddhists".

## 2. Use the community's own terms

| Prefer | Over | Note |
|:---|:---|:---|
| The name a group uses for itself | Outsiders' names | Latter-day Saints / The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints; Muslims (never "Mohammedans"); Sikhs; Baháʼís |
| "New religious movement" | "Cult" | Unless quoting, and say who said it |
| "Indigenous religions", the specific tradition's name | "Primitive", "animist" as a blanket label | Name the people and tradition |
| "Hebrew Bible" or "Tanakh" for the Jewish scriptures | "Old Testament" | "Old Testament" is right when writing about Christian use |
| "Myth" only in its technical sense, explained | "Myth" meaning false | "Story" or "account" is often clearer |
| BCE / CE | BC / AD | Neutral convention in scholarship; give a tradition's own era alongside when relevant (AH, AM, BE, Vikram Samvat) |

## 3. Quote with a source

Every scripture quotation names the text, the locus and the translation: "(Qur'an 2:256,
Saheeh International)", "(Matthew 5:9, NRSV)". Check the quotation against the text before
publishing: many widely shared "scripture" quotations are paraphrases or inventions, and
sayings attributed to the Buddha, Rumi or Confucius are often modern.

## Conventions

- **Capitalize** names of religions, their adherents, sacred texts, holy days and deity
  names as used by the tradition (God, Allah, Brahman, the Buddha). Lowercase generic
  "god" or "gods". Don't capitalize "scripture" generically.
- **Transliterate consistently**: pick a system (ALA-LC, or the house style) and keep to
  it; give diacritics at first use if dropping them later. *Qur'an* or *Quran*, *Muhammad*,
  *Shabbat*, *nirvana*, *Diwali*.
- **Honorifics**: report them when quoting, don't adopt them in your own voice
  (Muslim writers add "peace be upon him"; a neutral piece doesn't need to).
- **Holidays and practices**: describe what practitioners do and mean, not what it looks
  like to an outsider.

## Topics that need extra care

Violence and persecution, abuse in religious institutions, conversion, apostasy,
religion and politics, dress (hijab, turban, kippah), dietary law, sacred images (many
Muslims object to depictions of Muhammad), and restricted Indigenous knowledge. For each:
be accurate, attribute, avoid sensational framing, and ask whether an image or detail is
needed at all.

## Check before publishing

- Every faith claim attributed? Every scripture quotation checked against the text?
- Would a member of each tradition mentioned recognize the description?
- Any word in the piece a community would find insulting?
- Does the piece take a side on whether any religion is true? It shouldn't.
